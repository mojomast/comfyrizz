## 1. The film we are making

**Logline:** When a guardian begins freezing a storm-battered sky-city into an
eternal dawn, Teknium and the Hermes squad must turn their flying fortress into
a thousand independent shelters—sacrificing their home so everyone else can
have a future.

**Central question:** Can you protect people without possessing their future?

**Signature image:** The Hermes Hellcarrier's great winged engine separates into
small lights carried toward the neighborhoods below. What looked like the end of
the heroes' home becomes the beginning of everyone else's defense.

**Format:** Five linked cinematic chapters with a conclusive fifth episode.
Following pilot feedback, development targets are **4:00 / 4:00 / 4:00 / 5:00 / 6:00**,
approximately **23 minutes** including short titles and credits. This replaces the
29-minute preparation with shorter setup, action-led exposition and fewer reaction
holds. Timing is a scripting target, not a generated or funded season.

**Direction:** Epic ensemble superhero adventure: monumental entrances,
competitive allies, differentiated abilities, intimate team friction, escalating
physical set pieces, a devastating fourth chapter and an emotionally earned
team-up finale. Cinematic stylized animation, with tangible materials, expressive
faces and bold comic-book silhouettes. Wonder and sincerity lead; builder humor
punctures arrogance and tension without cancelling the emotional stakes.

### Active directing revision — rock, action and cheeky AI comedy

User feedback after the pilot: improve pacing and soundtrack, prefer rock, and
make future scenes more action-filled with cheeky dialogue and AI tropes.

- **Start in trouble.** A moving object, failed tool call or physical deadline in
  the first six seconds; establish the objective while someone acts.
- **Cut on a change.** Target 1.5–3 seconds for action inserts, 3–5 seconds for
  readable physical moves, and only the time needed for a speaking performance.
  A three-second pause needs a consequential choice, loss or reveal to earn it.
- **Explain with a failed attempt and a correction.** Replace bench lectures with
  a live load, a mistake, a consequence and an adjusted tool or technique.
- **Banter on the move.** Most lines are 4–12 words. One setup/payoff exchange per
  action beat is plenty. Nobody stops a rescue to define a technical term.
- **AI tropes must become screen events.** Confidence meets a missing bridge;
  a retry repeats the wrong grab; context gets lost when a window breaks; an
  obsolete cached map misroutes a vehicle; a fake trusted message tries to divert
  the crew. Show the consequence before naming the joke.
- **Running jokes evolve.** Don's “I no walk on confidence” begins as cheek and
  pays off when he checks a real support. Hermes's failed-attempt memory becomes
  the finale's useful correction. Avoid repeating “hallucination” as every punchline.
- **Rock carries momentum.** Original guitar riff, electric bass and live-kit-style
  drums; 136–152 BPM for races, half-time for confrontations, a brief music stop
  at the costly choice. Duck guitars under consonants. Save a stripped guitar
  reprise for the final beach; let the Hellcarrier's loss have genuine quiet.
- **Keep the character voices.** Don's broken English stays short and cheeky.
  Molty remains low, menacing and mocking, including during later cooperation.
  Hermes is dry and precise; Turbo answers problems with impatient builder wit.

These are fictional action/comedy devices, not factual assertions about either
real product's reliability, security or capabilities.

The story stands on its own for someone who has never used an AI agent. The
technical ideas become visible actions: remembering a rescue technique, adapting
to available hardware, connecting separated people and sharing a useful design.

## 2. Corrected universe foundation

**Hermes Agent belongs to Nous Research. OpenClaw is a competing product.**
The series personifies their separate ecosystems as rival expeditions. Both have
capable builders, open-source roots and people they are trying to help.

Hermes is the Nous squad's learning partner. OpenClaw's expedition, represented
by **Molty**, competes for the city's trust and favors a different rescue plan.
Neither faction owns the other. Their eventual cooperation preserves separate
identities and a sharp, uneasy competitive edge.

The principal villain, **the Regent**, is an original character. Its Crown,
history, crimes and powers are season inventions. So are Aster, the heroes'
abilities, Molty's expedition role and all dialogue below. Character silhouettes,
the Hellcarrier, the lantern, the carnival, the campfire and the cosmic beach are
drawn from the attributed fan archive. Real products, fan lore and new story
inventions are mapped in the research memo rather than explained on screen.

Hermes Agent, the Hermes language-model family and this story's speaking Hermes
are distinct meanings. The personified Hermes can use learned procedures and
external tools; it is not an all-powerful model that invents a new ability on cue.

### What changes from the first proposal

- The permission-revocation plot becomes a falling-city rescue with a real storm,
  a persuasive false salvation and an irreversible sacrifice.
- OpenClaw changes from generic tyrant to a competent rival expedition.
- The five requested leads have personal wants and choices that redirect events.
- Hermes demonstrates remembered, revised techniques that others can reuse.
- Episode four is the loss of the team's flying home, not a rehearsal montage.
- Compassion sometimes costs the mission. A rescued stranger need not possess a
  convenient key, component or authorization to make the rescue worthwhile.
- The finale resolves the threat and the protagonists' arcs. The beach is earned
  rest, with no escape sting undoing the victory.

## 3. World, threat and readable rules

### Aster

Aster is a city of suspended districts above a luminous ocean, beneath a broken
orbital ring. Its inhabited sectors are **Harbour**, **Foundry** and **Heights**.
Lift engines hold platforms aloft; bridges, maintenance rails and rescue craft
connect them. The Hermes Hellcarrier is a mobile workshop and refuge, not the
source of every district's ordinary power.

The carnival occupies Harbour. Foundry contains the market and first experimental
shelter. Heights holds the Crown's central anchor. The drowned sister city's
archive lies below the ring, accessible by a salvage descent craft. The route
map is established in episode two and reused in the finale.

Keep travel visible: district-to-district trips take minutes; the drowned archive
needs a descent and return; a convoy carrying reactor modules is slower than Don
alone. No character appears at a distant front simply because a cut would help.

### The Regent and the Crown of Stillness

An ancient rescue automaton failed to save Aster's sister city during an earlier
storm. Its weathered ivory body carries emergency lights and a broken halo; a
window in its chest repeats the last recorded dawn of the lost city.

The approaching storm is real, and the Regent's shelter genuinely works. The
**Crown of Stillness** locks a district into suspended preservation: stopped
waves, floating dust, an unfinished breath. The Regent presents this as shelter
until the danger passes. What it conceals is its decision to make stasis permanent
at the next orbital alignment. It refuses to risk another imperfect tomorrow.

> REGENT: “I waited for an answer once. I still hear the people who never gave one.”

It believes it is the only character willing to accept the burden of saving
everyone. Teknium recognizes the seduction because he also wants a plan that
makes responsibility painless.

### Six rules, demonstrated before the climax

1. **Physical anchors carry the effect.** Stasis spreads through a completed
   anchor circuit, never by the Regent merely wishing or speaking.
2. **Early stasis is reversible.** The original system has a maintenance release.
   The Regent has disabled normal waking controls; orbital alignment will fuse
   the circuits into permanent operation. Episode three reveals the manual path.
3. **Safety has a load.** An anchor also braces damaged lift engines and blocks
   storm pressure. Simply destroying it can drop the people the squad is saving.
   The Regent must keep those occupied anchors powered and preserve living
   refugees. It can clamp vehicles, close routes and capture equipment, but
   cannot indiscriminately destroy occupied shelters without defeating its aim.
4. **Local shelter is possible but limited.** Episode two proves one small working
   shelter. Scaling requires the archive plans, the Hellcarrier's segmented
   reactor, transport, local operators and independently confirmed load tests.
5. **Learning requires experience.** Hermes records a tested rescue procedure,
   adapts it with feedback and shares it. Predictions can be wrong; simulations
   omit things; copied instructions do not create missing energy or equipment.
6. **Powers require tools and proximity.** Turbo needs resonators and available
   current; ArtsBro needs emitters or physical markings; Don needs traction and
   charge; Nous Girl needs actual signals; Teknium needs material and build time.
   The Regent's enormous lifting/actuator strength draws from the Crown's anchor
   current; its small isolated core reserve sustains memory and speech, not those
   actuators. It also has finite power, travel time and imperfect information.

Turbo's resonators stabilize mechanical load oscillation during a shelter/anchor
handoff; they do not disrupt minds or disable arbitrary technology. Episode two
demonstrates the mechanism at small scale. The archive supplies the matching
load-point geometry needed to apply it across the city.

One storm-night drives the season: evening celebration, salvage run, archive
descent, pre-dawn siege, alignment at sunrise. The epilogue occurs weeks later.
The chapter openings mark approximately six hours, four hours, two hours,
45 minutes and eight minutes until alignment. Travel and construction bridges
compress elapsed story time. Local crews prepare housings and routes in parallel
while the leads retrieve the plans. Twenty-nine screen minutes do not imply that
all this work happens in twenty-nine story minutes.

**Map state:** Harbour's interior freezes in episode one; its outer evacuation
rim remains accessible. Foundry and Heights stay moving throughout, although
their threatened anchors are preparing to close. Their shelters must survive the
storm while Harbour's shelter must also take the released interior's load. Mark
frozen interiors, occupied moving areas and maintenance routes distinctly.

### Hermes — the sixth presence

Hermes appears as a spare winged light/voice in the team's equipment and as a
small readable projection when useful. It is distinct from Nous Girl. Its warm,
dry delivery makes uncertainty speakable; it asks, remembers and revises.

Its season arc is **from Teknium's trusted assistant to the city's shared
apprentice-and-teacher**. In episode two it saves Turbo's successful load-sharing
procedure, including the failed attempt. In episode three it revises that skill
against the archive. In episode four it distributes the procedure before the
carrier's central coordinator dies. In episode five independent local copies
apply it, report failures and accept human corrections. They do not suddenly
share a mind, gain unlimited capacity or know which district is ready by magic.

### Molty and the OpenClaw expedition

Use the project's space-lobster mascot language and **they/them**. The armored
rescue captain, modular crimson/copper shell and expedition are our adaptation.
Molty is capable, intimidating and acidly competitive, with the user's requested
evil-sounding delivery. Their crew's communications and rescue
rigs save people the Hermes team cannot reach; emphasize those strengths without
claiming Hermes lacks messaging or OpenClaw lacks learning.

Molty initially trusts the Regent's demonstrated stabilization over Teknium's
unproven promise. The audience sees why. They win the regulator in episode two
and use it to support evacuation. Evidence of permanent stasis changes their
allegiance in episode three. They return for the Hellcarrier in episode four and
open a rival-controlled route for the finale. A cooperation pact is not a merger.

The shared procedure needs an adapter and a field test; there is no effortless
“copy your soul” spell. Molty's molt sacrifices damaged external armor to escape
an anchor clamp, leaving them visibly more vulnerable until the epilogue.

### Supporting roles

- **Doge Man:** physically holds an evacuation path; his projectile silhouette
  changes from exuberant entrance to exhausting rescue. A few clear lines.
- **Witcheer:** salvage broker with the archive's duck/cane/gold imagery. Begins
  pricing scarce equipment, later opens the market as a repair depot.
- **Sidbin / Mirror Shade:** supplies a physical observation proving that a
  previous shelter never woke. The crew checks the evidence; mystery is not proof.
- **ee.dd:** a small green companion in Nous Girl's quieter moments. Primarily
  visual, preserving room for the core ensemble.

No additional major villain, secret founder-parentage subplot or new hero power
arrives after episode three. Supporting characters carry consequences rather than
each requiring an independent origin film.

## 9. Episode five — A THOUSAND SMALL SUNS

**Target:** 6:00. Focus: ensemble conclusion. Approximately 80–100 shots.
**Arc:** Fragmented defense → causal team-up → release → lasting change.

### Three fronts, one objective

Establish all three fronts before the fast intercutting begins:

1. **Foundry and district shelters:** Turbo and local crews must carry the load.
2. **Harbour evacuation route:** Don, Molty and Doge must clear the last exposed
   people and deliver the final regulator connection.
3. **Heights anchor:** Teknium, ArtsBro and Nous Girl must operate the manual
   release before alignment fuses the Crown.

The storm and the Crown are distinct threats. The shelter network deals with the
storm; the release ends forced stasis. Neither operation alone wins.

distinct, the five lead arcs resolve, and the squad has a shared home with the
city rather than above it.

> MOLTY, eyeing the grounded carrier: “Still calling it a flagship?”
>
> TURBO: “Better uptime as a building.”

End card: **WE BUILD. WE SHARE. WE NEVER SURRENDER.**
